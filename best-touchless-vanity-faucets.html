Frequently Asked Questions

Are touchless vanity faucets worth buying instead of a manual faucet?

Touchless vanity faucets help with hygiene since you never touch the handle with dirty hands, and they can save water by shutting off automatically. The tradeoff is a battery that needs periodic replacement and a sensor that occasionally misfires, staying on too long or not activating right away. Verified owner reviews we compared show reliability varies a lot by price point, and cheaper sensor models are more likely to have these problems than the manual faucets in this guide.

What is the difference between centerset and widespread vanity faucets?

Centerset faucets have all the holes drilled 4 inches apart and use a single unified base, like the LUFG and Moen Ronan in this guide. Widespread faucets, like the Delta Roe and Delta Nicoli, have the handles and spout as separate pieces connected underneath the sink, typically spaced 8 inches apart. Measure your vanity's existing hole spacing before buying either style, since they are not interchangeable without redrilling the counter.

How long should a bathroom faucet valve last?

Most ceramic disc valves in this guide, including the Delta and LUFG models, are rated for 500,000 cycles, which works out to well over a decade of typical household use. A valve rated lower than that, or one with no published cycle rating at all, is more likely to develop drips or turn stiff within a few years, based on the complaints in verified reviews of unrated competitors.

Can I install a widespread vanity faucet myself?

Yes, if your vanity already has 8-inch widespread holes drilled and you're comfortable working under a sink. The Delta faucets in this guide use a wrench-free, quick-connect hose that clicks into place, which cuts out one of the more frustrating steps in a DIY widespread install. Budget an hour for a first attempt, and turn off the water supply valves under the sink before you start.
