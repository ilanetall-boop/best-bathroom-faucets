Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Check your hole spacing first: Widespread sinks need a faucet built for an 8-inch spread, while centerset sinks need a compact 4-inch model. Mixing these up means a return trip to the store.
  • Price doesn't always track quality: Our cheapest pick costs $37.98 and our most expensive costs $139.00, and the extra money mostly buys brand reputation, not a fundamentally different sensor.
  • Finish affects daily maintenance: Matte black hides water spots and fingerprints far better than chrome, which shows every drip until you wipe it down.
  • Sensor faucets still need power: Most touchless faucets run on batteries, an AC adapter, or both, so factor that into your install plan before committing to one.
  • Brass construction is the norm: Every faucet in this roundup uses a brass body with a finish coating, which is standard for durability at these price points.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do touchless sink faucets work?

A touchless sink faucet uses an infrared motion sensor near the spout to detect your hands and start or stop the water automatically. Most models run on batteries, an AC adapter, or both, so the sensor keeps working even during a power outage if you keep a battery backup installed.

Are touchless sink faucets worth the extra cost?

For most households, yes. A touchless faucet cuts down on the germs you spread onto handles with wet or soapy hands, and it stops the drip-and-run habit that wastes water at a manual tap. Prices in this roundup range from $37.98 to $139.00, so you don't need to spend a premium to get the hands-free benefit.

Does a touchless faucet fit any bathroom sink?

It depends on your hole spacing. Widespread sinks need a faucet built for a wider spread, like our top pick's 8-inch spacing, while centerset sinks need a faucet sized for that closer spacing, like the 4-inch model in this list. Check your current faucet's hole layout before you buy a replacement.

Can I install a touchless faucet myself?

Most touchless faucets install the same way as a standard faucet, using the same supply lines and mounting hardware, with the addition of a battery pack or low-voltage adapter for the sensor. If you're comfortable replacing a manual faucet, the touchless version adds only a small amount of extra wiring work.
