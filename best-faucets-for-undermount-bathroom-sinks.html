Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of faucet works with an undermount bathroom sink?

Any faucet whose hole count matches your counter, since the sink itself has no holes to drill. Undermount counters are typically set up for a single hole, a 4-inch centerset with three holes, or an 8-inch widespread with three holes. Measure your counter before ordering rather than assuming based on your old faucet.

Why does spout height matter more for undermount sinks?

Because the bowl sits lower than the counter surface, a short or flat spout can send water against the front rim of the sink instead of down into the basin. Spout heights around 5 inches or taller, like the faucets in this guide, generally clear a recessed undermount bowl without splashing.

Are matte black faucets harder to maintain than chrome?

Matte black shows mineral spots more visibly than chrome because the dark finish contrasts with light-colored water deposits. A quick wipe with a dry microfiber cloth after use prevents most buildup, and a PVD-coated matte black finish, like several picks in this guide, resists chipping and fading far longer than a painted coating.

Can I install a faucet on an undermount sink myself?

Most of the faucets in this guide are listed as DIY-friendly, with pre-attached supply lines and standard 1/2-inch connections. A typical install runs 15 to 60 minutes with a basin wrench and plumber's tape. Shut off both supply valves under the sink before you start, and confirm your hole spacing matches the faucet before you open the box.
