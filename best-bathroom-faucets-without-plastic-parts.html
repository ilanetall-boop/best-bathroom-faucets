Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Weight tells you a lot. A brass-body faucet feels heavy in the hand. If a single-handle model weighs under a pound, it almost certainly hides a plastic or zinc core.
  • The cartridge matters as much as the body. Look for a ceramic-disc cartridge. That is the valve that controls the water, and a ceramic one outlasts the plastic-seat versions that start dripping within a year or two.
  • Metal supply lines beat plastic connectors. The included plastic hoses on cheap faucets crack first. Braided stainless supply lines cost a few dollars and remove that failure point.
  • Match the hole spacing before you buy. Centerset faucets need holes 4 inches apart, widespread models need 8 inches, and single-hole faucets need one opening. Measure your sink first.
  • Finish affects upkeep, not durability. Brushed nickel and spot-defense chrome hide water spots better than polished chrome, so you wipe them down less often.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a bathroom faucet has plastic parts?

Check the product listing for the body material. The best bathroom faucets without plastic parts list a solid brass or stainless steel body and metal handles. If the spec sheet says the faucet weighs under a pound, or the description mentions a zinc or ABS core, expect plastic inside. A heavier faucet, roughly two to four pounds for a single-handle model, usually signals a brass body.

Are all-metal faucets worth the extra cost?

For a fixture you use several times a day for a decade or more, yes. Brass bodies resist cracking at the threads, handle hot water without warping, and hold up to hard-water minerals better than plastic. Our top pick, the Pfister Sonterra, costs $79 and carries a lifetime finish and function warranty, which spreads the cost to a few dollars a year over its life.

Do bathroom faucets without plastic parts last longer?

In our experience they do. The parts that fail first on a cheap faucet are the plastic supply connectors and the plastic cartridge housing, both of which crack under heat cycling and water pressure. A brass body with a ceramic-disc cartridge removes those weak points, so the faucet drips less and survives more handle turns before it needs service.

What size faucet fits my bathroom sink?

Count the holes and measure the spacing. A single-hole sink takes a single-hole faucet like the Pfister Willa. Three holes spaced 4 inches apart take a centerset such as the Sonterra, and three holes spaced 8 inches apart take a widespread faucet like the Pfister Courant. Measure center to center between the outer holes before you order.

Can I keep my brass faucet but replace the plastic hoses?

Yes, and it is a smart, cheap upgrade. The plastic supply lines that ship with many faucets are the first thing to fail. A metal connector kit like our $5.49 budget pick swaps them out in a few minutes. Confirm the thread size matches your faucet tailpieces and shutoff valves first so the fittings seal without adapters.
