Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Count your sink holes first. A standard 4-inch centerset sink has three holes; a single-hole sink has one. Most single-handle faucets here include a deck plate so they fit either, but a widespread sink with 8-inch holes needs a widespread faucet.
  • Brass beats zinc. A solid brass body resists corrosion far longer than the zinc-alloy used in the cheapest faucets. Every pick we recommend uses a brass body, which is the single biggest predictor of how long the faucet lasts.
  • Ceramic disc cartridges are what you want. The cartridge controls the water, and ceramic disc designs outlast rubber-washer valves by years. Cartridge failure, not the spout, is the usual reason a cheap faucet starts to drip.
  • Finish is about upkeep, not just looks. Brushed nickel hides spots, brushed gold runs warmer but shows soap film, and polished chrome shines but streaks. Four of our seven picks use brushed nickel for that reason.
  • You can stay well under $200. Our top pick costs $53.99 and the cheapest faucet here is $23.98. Spending the full $199 on the Delta mostly buys a brand name and a lifetime warranty, not better daily performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I spend on a bathroom faucet?

You can buy a solid bathroom faucet for $25 to $60. Every faucet in this roundup of the best bathroom faucets under $200 falls below that ceiling, and our top pick costs $53.99. Spending more than $100 mostly buys you a brand name, a heavier brass body, and a longer warranty, as you see with the $199.90 Delta Nicoli. Below about $25 you start losing ceramic disc cartridges and metal hardware, which is where leaks and stripped handles begin.

Will a single-hole faucet fit my three-hole sink?

Yes, if you add a deck plate. Most single-handle faucets, including our Hurran top pick, ship with an optional escutcheon plate that covers the two outer holes of a standard 4-inch centerset sink. If your sink has 8-inch widespread holes, you need a widespread faucet or a deck plate rated for that spread. Measure the distance between the centers of your outer holes before you buy.

Is brushed nickel or brushed gold easier to keep clean?

Brushed nickel hides water spots and fingerprints better than any other finish, which is why four of the seven faucets here use it. Brushed gold, like the finish on the Delta Nicoli, looks warmer but shows soap film sooner and needs a quick wipe most days. Polished chrome, such as the Peerless, is the shiniest and the hardest to keep streak-free. Pick brushed nickel if you hate cleaning.

Can I install one of these faucets myself?

Most people can. A single-handle faucet like the Hurran or ryuwanku takes about 30 to 45 minutes with a basin wrench, plumber's tape, and patience. The main snag is reach: budget faucets often ship with short supply hoses, so if you have a deep vanity, buy a pair of longer braided supply lines before you start. Two-handle faucets such as the IUERASD and Peerless take a little longer because you connect two valves instead of one.
